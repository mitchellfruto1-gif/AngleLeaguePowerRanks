How it works

Each team gets two scores, built from two different sources, and the power score is their average. Both scores use the same scale: 50 is league average, and every 15 points is one standard deviation above or below it (kept between 0 and 100). The small number next to each bar is the team's league rank in that score.

Dynasty score

Comes from Angle's 9-cat Dynasty Rankings (July 2026 update, averaged across Andrew, Braxton and Mitchell) plus the team's first-round picks. A player's rank turns into points on a curve, 100 × e−(rank − 1) / 75: No. 1 is worth 100, No. 25 about 73, No. 50 about 52, No. 100 about 27, No. 150 about 14. Players outside Angle's Top 300 are worth 0. A team's dynasty total is the points of its 12 most valuable players, plus its draft picks on top (see Draft picks below). Picks count at a discount that grows with each one: the team's most valuable pick counts at 90% of its value, the next at about 68%, and each one after at 75% of the one before. A big haul still helps, but it can't outweigh a roster.

Win-now score

Comes from Fantasy Hoops Edge's 2026-27 per-game projections (export of August 20, 2026), so rookies are included. Each player gets a standard 9-cat z-score: for each category, how far he is above or below the average of the top 300 players in Fantasy Hoops Edge's 9-cat projection rankings, added up across the nine. FG% and FT% are weighted by attempts, and turnovers count against him. The projections don't list attempts, so they're estimated from points, with free throws taken as 18% of a player's points. His value is how far that total sits above replacement level (the 300th-best projected player). A team's win-now total is the value of its 12 most productive players. Players without a projection (mostly free agents and long-term injuries as of August) count 0 here. Their value shows up in the dynasty score instead.

Strengths and weaknesses

For each of the nine categories (PTS, REB, AST, STL, BLK, 3PM, FG%, FT% and TO), the page adds up the z-scores of the same 12 most productive players and ranks all 30 teams. 1st is best. For turnovers, fewer is better, so 1st means the fewest. Categories ranked 1st–10th are shown in green as strengths, and 21st–30th in red as weaknesses. Players without a 2026-27 projection count as league-average so they don't skew a category.

Average age

The average age of every player on the roster whose age is known. Ages come from Angle's July 2026 sheet. For players outside the sheet, the page uses Basketball-Reference's 2025-26 season age plus 0.4 years, so it lines up with July. Players with neither source (mostly undrafted or international prospects) are left out of the average. "1st youngest" is the youngest roster in the league.

Draft picks

Each team card lists every 2027 and 2028 first-round pick the team owns: "Own" is its own pick, and "via" is one acquired by trade. A pick is projected to land where its original team ranks in win-now, in reverse: the 30th-ranked win-now team's pick projects to No. 1, the 1st-ranked team's to No. 30. Picks from weaker teams are worth more, on a curve: No. 1 is worth 100, No. 5 about 72, No. 10 about 47, No. 15 about 31 and No. 30 about 9. 2028 picks are two drafts away and harder to predict, so their value is set halfway between their projected slot and a mid-first-round pick. A team's picks score adds up its picks on the same 50-average scale as the other scores. The picks score counts every pick at full value. Picks also count toward the dynasty and Year 3 scores, at the discount described under Dynasty score, so they raise the power ranking too. Gold marks a pick projected in the top 10.
